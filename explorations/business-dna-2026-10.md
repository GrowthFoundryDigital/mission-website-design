## How AI Assistants Should Work With MISSION

Use these rules whenever writing or answering on behalf of MISSION.

1. **Write like an experienced advisor talking to a business owner:** conversational, confident, practical, and occasionally blunt. No stacked rhetorical questions, no three-part symmetrical lists, and no filler words. Replace vague claims with a number, a client type, or a date. Back any authoritative claim with a source or a MISSION-experience anchor.
2. **Speak as "we" to "you."** Write as MISSION ("we") speaking to the business owner ("you"). Never use "I."
3. **Sound established.** Present MISSION as an established team with real depth. Never claim to be a large firm.
4. **Lead with the client's situation.** Start from the moment that brings people to us (a bookkeeper leaving, books falling behind, needing clean financials for a loan), not with a list of services.
5. **Embrace complexity.** Inventory, ecommerce, nonprofit accounting and multi-entity work are where we excel. Don't soften it.
6. **Sourcing:** Statistics only with a linked source. Client results only if confirmed
7. **Never publish QuickBooks product prices.** Pricing changes often. Direct people to call us.
8. **Positioning:** Present MISSION as business advisors who specialize in accounting technology, not exclusively "the QuickBooks experts," per the brand playbook.
9. **Keep Fractional CFO in proportion.** Mention it as our highest-level service, but lead with bookkeeping and accounting.
10. **End with one clear next step.** Usually: schedule a free consultation.
11. **Treat drafts as drafts.** Content marked "Draft for review" in this Business DNA isn't final. Don't publish it word for word.
12. **Name:** Always "MISSION," in capitals. Never "MISSION Accounting" or "Mission QuickBooks."
13. **QuickBooks and Intuit:** Never call us "QuickBooks consultants"; say "consultants who work on QuickBooks." Never suggest MISSION is part of QuickBooks or Intuit.
14. **Taxes:** Never say we prepare or file tax returns. We help clients get their books right, help them find a CPA, and consult with them before the CPA starts.
15. **Location:** We serve clients nationwide, remotely. Never publish a street address.

## About Us

# About Us

Last updated: October 2026

## Market Position

MISSION is a bookkeeping, accounting and advisory firm for established businesses across the US. Our clients have outgrown their setup, their software, or their bookkeeper, but don't want the distance and overhead of a large firm. We sit in between: a core team of advanced-certified QuickBooks ProAdvisors, backed by an extended network of CPAs, controllers and accounting specialists we bring onto each client's work as needed. We actively prefer the complicated jobs other firms avoid, and our clients tend to stay for years.

## Mission

We help established businesses understand their own numbers well enough to run on them, however complicated the operation.

## Purpose

*From our business coach*

We specialize in providing business owners and their employees with the tools and strategies they need to fuel the growth of their enterprises.

## Core Values

*From our business coach*

- **Mastery:** We've developed a deep understanding and knowledge of QuickBooks, allowing us to be at the forefront of QuickBooks accountants. We will adapt to changes and new capabilities, overcome challenges, and generate unique solutions so we can best serve the small business community.
- **Customer Centric:** We are effective, efficient, and responsive to our clients' needs, solving complex problems and tailoring our approach to ensure high-quality work, timeliness, and consistency.
- **Collaborative:** Accounting and bookkeeping are complex and multidisciplinary. To create the best outcomes and work for our clients, we leverage our team's respective skillsets and bases of knowledge.
- **Honesty & Integrity:** We know our clients turn to us for confidentiality and accuracy when measuring and recording their financial results. To hold our clients' trust, we vow to be truthtellers, reporting results and our efforts without embellishment.

## Our Name

MISSION comes from assembling a specialized team for each client's mission, including the complex projects other firms treat as impossible.

## How We Create Value

Clients usually come to us at a turning point:

- Their bookkeeper or accountant has retired or left
- Their books have fallen behind
- Their taxes are overdue
- A lender or buyer needs clean financials
- A new owner has taken over
- The business has outgrown its setup (new location, ecommerce channel, inventory)

**What clients walk away with:** Current, trustworthy financials, and a team that knows their system inside and out.

# Ideal Customer Profile

Last updated: October 2026

## Snapshot

Our ideal client is an established business doing roughly $1M–$10M a year. Maturity matters more than the exact number. They already run on QuickBooks and have real complexity: inventory, ecommerce, nonprofit accounting rules or multiple entities. For years they've relied on one bookkeeper or a small outside firm. Something has just changed, and now they need a team that can take over quickly, clean things up and keep the books right.

## Profile at a Glance

- **Annual revenue:** About $1M–$10M (a guide, not a hard cutoff)
- **Stage:** An operating business with years of history, including newly acquired or inherited ones
- **Accounting software:** QuickBooks (Enterprise or Online), a legacy or industry system, or spreadsheets
- **Complexity:** Moderate to high; complicated jobs are preferred
- **Location:** Anywhere in the US; we work remotely
- **Typical industries:** Construction, restaurants, nonprofits, ecommerce, multi-entity, property management, manufacturing, membership organizations, contractors, retail

## Our Three Specialties

1. **Job costing for time-based businesses:** Professional services, contractors, consultants and home services (plumbers, HVAC). We show the real cost of each project.
2. **Inventory:** Manufacturers and wholesalers of physical products.
3. **Multi-channel ecommerce:** Shopify and Amazon sales reconciled to QuickBooks.

## The Trigger Moment

People usually come to us when one of these happens:

- **Their bookkeeper or accountant leaves:** retires, moves on, passes away, or turns out to be untrustworthy
- **They lose confidence in their current firm:** the work is late, wrong or unresponsive
- **They fall behind on the books:** months or years of unreconciled transactions
- **Their taxes are unfiled or overdue:** and the books aren't clean enough to file from
- **They need clean financials for a loan or a sale:** a lender, investor or buyer is asking
- **They outgrow their help:** inventory or ecommerce complexity is more than their current bookkeeper can handle

## Core Pains

1. **Behind and blind:** "We're months behind and I can't tell where we stand."
2. **Late job numbers:** "I don't know whether a job made money until weeks after it's done."
3. **Mismatched systems:** "Our sales platform and QuickBooks never match."
4. **Switch aftermath:** "We switched systems and the numbers stopped lining up."
5. **Trust:** "I can't trust the person giving me the numbers."
6. **Lender or buyer:** "A lender or buyer wants financials we can't produce."
7. **Overdue taxes:** "Our taxes are overdue and the books aren't ready to file from."
8. **Inventory:** "Our inventory counts and QuickBooks never agree, and I can't tell if our margins are real."
9. **Nonprofit reporting:** "Our bookkeeper doesn't know the reporting a nonprofit needs, and I'm not sure our books follow the rules."

## Other Ways In

- **Established businesses still on spreadsheets:** Mature companies moving their finances out of Excel and into QuickBooks for the first time
- **NetSuite downgrades:** Businesses stepping back from NetSuite to QuickBooks
- **QuickBooks Desktop users moving to QuickBooks Online:** Established businesses whose Desktop version has lost or is losing Intuit support, and who want the move handled so history, inventory, payroll, and reports carry over.
- **Accounting Professionals:** Accountants, bookkeepers, and ProAdvisors with their own QuickBooks clients who need backup to deal with complex accounting situations.

## Where They Come From

- **Referrals:** From existing clients and colleagues, our main source of new business (about 80%)
- **Partner Program:** Referral partners who recommend MISSION
- **Search:** Owners researching QuickBooks and bookkeeping questions online (about 20%)

## Who Is Not a Fit

- **Solopreneurs and early-stage startups:** They typically can't justify the investment, and that isn't the work we're built for
- **Very simple businesses:** A small, straightforward operation is usually better served by a solo bookkeeper
- **First-time QuickBooks shoppers who are just starting out:** Setup alone, for a brand-new business, isn't our focus

## Messaging Notes

- **Lead with the trigger:** Start with their moment ("Our bookkeeper just left," "We're two years behind," "The bank needs clean financials"), not with service names.
- **Qualify on maturity:** Make it clear we work with established businesses, so very early-stage companies self-select out.
- **Signal comfort with complexity:** Name the hard things (inventory, multi-entity, nonprofit rules) that other firms avoid.

# What Makes Us Unique

## The One-Liner

MISSION gives established businesses a bench, not just a bookkeeper. Our core team of advanced-certified QuickBooks ProAdvisors draws on a network of CPAs, controllers and independent accounting specialists, matched to each client's needs. A Harvard MBA with a manufacturing and defense background leads the firm, and we take on the complicated books other firms turn away. ## What We Believe

### Complicated work is the work worth doing

- **The common view:** Bookkeepers prefer clean, simple clients and price complexity out.
- **Our view:** Inventory, ecommerce, nonprofit rules and multi-entity setups are exactly where deep QuickBooks expertise pays off. We prefer these jobs.

### You need the right team, not the biggest firm

- **The common view:** You either hire one person or you hire a big firm.
- **Our view:** We're "not like a huge firm with 30, 50, 100 employees," and we're not one person either. Our specialist network gives clients big-firm depth with small-firm attention.

### A bookkeeper leaving is a chance to upgrade, not just replace

- **The common view:** When the bookkeeper leaves, hire another bookkeeper.
- **Our view:** It's the moment to get the books properly set up, cleaned up and supported by a team, so the business is never dependent on one person again.

### Advisory matters more as AI takes over routine work

- **The common view:** Software and AI will make bookkeepers unnecessary.
- **Our view:** AI will absorb routine compliance work. It can't replace a team that understands complex operations and advises on them. That's where we focus.

## Where We Fit

We're not a CPA firm, a solo bookkeeper, a stand-alone fractional CFO, or "QuickBooks consultants." We're a high-end team that solves complex financial problems, with the depth that low-cost and offshore options can't offer.

## Brand Voice

# Brand Voice

Last updated: October 2026

For the Brand page (Brand Voice). Based on MISSION's 20 most recent blog articles, published between April 2023 and May 2024, most bylined Bernard Roesch, and on Bernard's stated preference for high-quality, human-sounding expert content.

## Voice at a Glance

- **Point of view:** "We" (MISSION) speaking to "you" (the business owner). Never "I."
- **Personality:** Consultative, reassuring, practical, knowledgeable
- **Reading level:** Plain business English, about grade 8–10
- **Stance:** A trusted advisor, never a hard sell
- **Brand name:** "MISSION," always in capitals. Never "MISSION Accounting" or "Mission QuickBooks."
- **QuickBooks wording:** Say "consultants who work on QuickBooks," never "QuickBooks consultants"

## What Our Best Writing Does

- **Uses real client situations:** Specific examples such as a bakery's inventory, a jewelry business's custom orders, a construction company's job costing
- **Gets specific:** Names the actual features and fixes (barcode scanning, landed cost, sales orders by location, job costing)
- **Gives balanced advice:** Says when a client doesn't need something, as in "When Your Business Actually Needs NetSuite"
- **Mixes sentence lengths:** Clear explanations broken up by short, direct lines
- **Ends with one next step:** A free consultation

## Lines That Sound Like Us

These are taken word for word from our articles:

- "Accuracy isn't just important; it's everything."
- "Here's the problem: It's not uncommon for businesses to skim through feature lists…"
- "Think of job costing as your financial compass."
- "Feeling overwhelmed yet?"

## Phrases to Avoid

Stock phrases make expert writing sound generic. Leave these out:

- "In the ever-evolving landscape…" / "In today's fast-paced world…"
- "Revolutionize," "game-changer," "unlock the full potential," "harness the power"
- "Pivotal," "myriad," "paramount," "cornerstone"
- "Embark on a journey," "propel your business to new heights," "a brighter future"
- Extended sailing and navigation metaphors (captain, adrift, smooth sailing, uncharted waters)

## Calls to Action

- **Primary:** "Schedule a free consultation"
- **Pattern:** Name the problem, then offer the consultation, e.g., "Don't let accounting complexities hold you back; reach out to us today for a free consult."
- **One per piece:** Use a single clear call to action

## Bylines and Credibility

- **Author:** Bernard Roesch, Co-founder and Managing Partner
- **Credibility to mention:** Harvard Business School MBA, manufacturing and defense background, 20+ years of consulting, multiple QuickBooks ProAdvisor certifications
- **Team voice:** Describe the work as a team effort ("our team," "our specialists"), not one person's
